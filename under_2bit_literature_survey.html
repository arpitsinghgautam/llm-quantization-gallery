1 Introduction & scope

Quantization compresses a model by storing its weights in fewer bits. Mainstream deployment has settled on 4-bit weights (GPTQ, AWQ) as a near-lossless sweet spot. Sub-2-bit quantization is the frontier below that:

1 bit — binary

Weights in {−1, +1}. ~16× smaller than FP16; matmul becomes pure sign-flips.

~1.58 bits — ternary

Weights in {−1, 0, +1} (log₂3 ≈ 1.58). The added zero enables feature filtering — the field's most important design choice.

< 1 bit — sub-1-bit

Factorization/codebook tricks where individual weights no longer have an independent representation (down to 0.1 bits).

The prize & the peril

Ternary/binary matmuls reduce to additions — order-of-magnitude energy savings. But naive binarization of a pretrained LLM collapses it below random.

Two structural facts shape every method here:

  1. The scale tailwind. Larger models tolerate fewer bits per weight — the FP16-vs-low-bit gap shrinks with size. Strong results cluster at ≥ 3 B parameters.
  2. The central fork. Either bake low precision into training for best quality, or convert an existing FP16 checkpoint cheaply. This fork organizes the whole field.

The arc of the field, 2023 → 2026

Two macro-trends: the pivot from binary → ternary in early 2024, and the 2026 move to push activations low-bit too.

3 / 4 Two converging lineages

The central fork produces two families. Train-from-scratch / QAT leads on quality but can't touch existing checkpoints. PTQ converts any FP16 model cheaply and is closing the gap fast.

Highlights from the train-from-scratch lineage

BitNet (2023) introduced BitLinear and showed billion-scale 1-bit LLMs follow the same scaling laws as FP16, crushing every post-hoc baseline (6.7 B: 17.07 PPL vs GPTQ's 1032 at the same bits). BitNet b1.58 (2024) added the ternary {0} state and reached FP16 parity at 3 B (9.91 vs 10.04 PPL) while 2.71× faster and 3.55× lighter. Spectra/TriLM proved a ternary 3.9 B model matches FP16 at 5.9× fewer bits; MatMul-free LM removed matrix multiplication entirely; and BitNet b1.58 2B4T shipped the first open 2 B/4T-token ternary model (avg 54.19, beating INT4 PTQ at lower memory). OneBit and LittleBit bring this quality to pretrained models via sign-value / latent-factorization + distillation — LittleBit reaching an unprecedented 0.1 bits/weight.

Highlights from the post-training lineage

PB-LLM (2023) was first to binarize LLMs and to show naive binarization collapses them — its fix was to keep a salient fraction high-bit. BiLLM (2024) reached usable ~1.1-bit with no training via residual binarization. SAGE-PTQ (2026) attacks the overlooked cost of the scales themselves (0.004 bit/weight), claiming near-FP16 at ~1 bit. The newest wave — TWLA (ternary W + 4-bit A), BWLA (1-bit W + 6-bit A), LBLLM (W(1+1)A4), Spectral-LLM (2-bit, Hadamard) — uses orthogonal rotations to make both weights and activations quantization-friendly at once, finally moving activations low-bit for real end-to-end speedup.

7 Open challenges & future directions

1 · Activations are the wall

Most methods keep activations at INT8 or FP16. True W1A4 is only just emerging (TWLA, BWLA, LBLLM) and still degrades at A4.

2 · Hardware gates the gains

Nearly every speedup needs custom kernels (bitnet.cpp, BitBLAS) or non-commodity hardware. Commodity GPUs have no native W1.58A8 path.

3 · PTQ ≈ from-scratch?

2026 PTQ claims near-FP16 at ~1 bit (SAGE-PTQ 5.87). If replicated this is field-changing — but these are recent single-team preprints.

4 · Small / new models are harder

Parity needs scale (≥ 3 B); newer dense models (LLaMA-3, Qwen3) degrade more at the same bit-width.

5 · Evaluation gaps

Subset perplexity, single seeds, limited baselines (e.g. Spectral-LLM vs only AutoRound). The field needs a standardized sub-2-bit benchmark.

6 · Theory lags practice

Why native 1-bit training works at scale, and why closed-form rotations recover learned-rotation gains, remain open.

Conclusion. In ~3 years the field went from "binarization destroys LLMs" (PB-LLM, 2023) to ternary models that match FP16 from scratch (BitNet b1.58 / 2B4T) and post-training methods that approach FP16 at ~1 bit (SAGE-PTQ, BWLA, TWLA). The decisive ideas: the ternary {0} state, saliency-aware mixed precision, sign-value / factorized representations, and orthogonal rotations that make weights and activations quantization-friendly at once. The binding constraints now are low-bit activations and hardware support — the algorithms are increasingly ahead of the silicon.
